The road map

Your 5 steps, from permit to pavement.

Tap a step to open it. Tap the circle when you finish it. Your progress stays on this phone.

0 of 5 done
  1. DocumentsPack your Go-Bag
    • Proof of identity and lawful presence. For US citizens, a US passport or certified US birth certificate.
    • Your Social Security number. The physical card isn't required.
    • One proof of your address, or two if you want a REAL ID
    • Driver's Ed proof: your enrollment contract or completion certificate. Parent-taught families bring the Service Oklahoma-approved affidavit and the course purchase receipt.

    Under 18? A parent or legal guardian comes with you. If they can't, bring a notarized Parent Authorization form.

  2. Parent-taught familiesGet your course approved

    Doing Parent-Taught Driver Education (PTDE)? Apply with Service Oklahoma so your course is approved. At your permit visit, bring the approved Parent-Taught affidavit and your course purchase receipt.

    Taking Driver Ed at a school instead? Skip to step 3.

  3. Written testPass the knowledge exam
    • 20 questions. Get 15 right to pass.
    • Heavy on school bus rules and the 3-second rule
    • Permit fee is $42.50. Each failed retake costs $4.
    • A Driver Ed completion certificate can waive the test. Parent-taught Driver Ed can't.

    Study the real Oklahoma Driver Manual. Practice tests alone won't cover it.

  4. 180 daysPractice your 50 hours
    • Hold your permit at least 180 days
    • Drive 50 hours with a licensed driver who is 21 or older and has been licensed at least 2 years. At least 10 of those hours at night.
    • Permit driving hours are 5am to 10pm
    • Finish the free Work Zone Safe course online. It's required if you're under 18.
    • Keep a clean record with no traffic convictions

    Empty lots first. Talk through what you see as you drive. No yelling, ever.

  5. Drive testPass the inspection and drive test
    • Your car gets a quick check first, including a valid license plate, working lights and turn signals, and a safe windshield
    • Bring proof of auto insurance
    • Expect turns, backing up, parking on a hill and parallel parking
    • You get 3 tries. After that, one try every 30 days.

    Calm, confident, ready. You've done the hours. Trust them.

Rules of the roadThe ones the test asks about most
Signal
At least 100 feet, or 1/3 of a block, before you turn, slow down or stop
Following distance
3 seconds. Make it 4 in bad weather.
Speed limits
Unless posted: 75 mph on controlled-access highways, 80 on turnpikes, 65 on undivided state highways, 55 on county roads. Cities set their own, so always obey the signs.
4-way stop
First to arrive goes first. A tie? The driver on the left yields to the right.
School bus
Flashing red lights or a STOP sign means traffic stops in both directions. On a divided highway, oncoming traffic doesn't have to stop.
Emergency vehicle
Pull over to the right edge, clear of intersections, and stop until it has passed

Parent co-pilot tipsFor the grown-up in the passenger seat
  • Start in an empty parking lot. Master the basics before traffic.
  • Use commentary driving. Your teen says out loud what they see and plan to do.
  • Keep a no-correction zone for small stuff. Talk it through after you park.
  • If voices rise, the lesson ends. Try again tomorrow.
  • Calm comes first. Try box breathing together: in for 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4.
